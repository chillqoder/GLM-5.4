# Vibe Coding Studio — Creative Website Brief

Build a complete website for a studio that provides vibe coding services: custom websites, web applications, business automation, integrations, MVPs, and SaaS products. Clients bring an idea or a business problem; the studio turns it into working software through AI-assisted development, design judgment, and practical execution.

The website itself must demonstrate the studio's capabilities. It should make a visitor curious enough to explore, confident enough to trust the team, and interested enough to start a project.

This is a creativity test. Invent the studio's name, identity, voice, positioning, visual concept, content, and commercial presentation. Make those decisions yourself. The requirements below define the experience and the quality bar; the creative solution is entirely yours.

## Eight sections, one coherent experience

Create exactly eight main content sections. Their purpose, names, order, composition, and connections are yours to invent. Navigation and footer do not count as additional sections.

Together, the sections must communicate what the studio builds, why someone should hire it, what a collaboration delivers, and how to get in touch. Cover the service range naturally rather than treating it as a mandatory list of identical cards. Invent compelling, specific copy with a recognizable voice. If you include demonstration projects, identify them honestly as concepts rather than fabricated client work.

Creative presentation must support a clear commercial purpose. A prospective client should understand the offer without having to decode the visual concept.

## Real-time 3D that travels and transforms

Make real-time 3D a central part of the entire website experience, extending well beyond the opening screen. Create an original 3D presence that moves between sections and changes as the visitor progresses. Its movement, transformations, and relationship to the content should connect the eight sections into a continuous visual journey.

You decide what this 3D presence is, how it looks, how it evolves, and what those changes mean. Do not default to unrelated scenes in separate boxes or a static object behind a headline. The visitor should perceive continuity as the 3D travels through the page, takes on different states, and interacts with the surrounding composition.

Scrolling and user interaction must meaningfully influence the 3D experience. Coordinate it with typography, layout, and transitions so that the page feels deliberately choreographed. Build actual geometry rendered in real time, not a video or a sequence of flat images presented as 3D.

## Contemporary digital art direction

The website must feel like an ambitious contemporary digital studio: expressive, technologically fluent, visually confident, and carefully designed. Develop an original visual language shared by the interface, 3D, imagery, typography, and motion.

Use bright, saturated color with a substantial presence across the composition. Choose the palette yourself. A dark page with a single neon accent, a mostly white page with one brand color, or a washed-out pastel treatment does not satisfy this requirement.

Typography is a major part of the design. Choose distinctive contemporary fonts and use scale, spacing, hierarchy, and composition with intent. The type should contribute personality while remaining readable. Select the actual fonts and typographic approach yourself; there is no prescribed pairing or layout.

## Motion with character

Create polished animation throughout the experience, including transitions between sections and responses to user interaction. Establish a consistent motion language that makes the website feel alive and reinforces the creative concept.

Decide the animation techniques, pacing, and behavior yourself. Motion should reward exploration and help connect the experience without delaying access to content, obstructing controls, or making navigation frustrating. Give the visitor something worth discovering beyond the first screen.

## Original generated imagery

Use your image generation tool to create original imagery for the website. Determine its subjects, style, placement, and relationship to the 3D yourself. All assets should share the same art direction and feel integrated into the composition.

No stock photography, hotlinked images, placeholders, or code-drawn graphics passed off as generated images. Generated imagery complements the real-time 3D requirement; it does not replace it. If no image generation tool is available, disclose that before building rather than silently substituting something else.

## Creative freedom, strong judgment

Do not ask for a predetermined visual concept. Choose your own. No particular object, character, metaphor, scene, font, palette, section layout, camera path, or transformation is prescribed.

Avoid the familiar AI-generated landing-page formula: interchangeable copy, repetitive cards, default bento grids, decorative wireframe spheres, glowing blobs, reflexive glassmorphism, generic violet-to-blue gradients, and empty claims about transforming ideas. Do not use glossy robots or glowing brains as shortcuts for communicating AI.

Aim for an unusual, cohesive website with its own identity. Originality must come from thoughtful art direction and execution. Every visual decision should belong to this studio, and every section should contribute something distinct.

## Delivery and verification

Deliver a finished website that opens from `index.html` in this folder and runs locally with no build step. Include the required assets and make the interface work across desktop and mobile. Adapt the 3D and motion to the device, respect reduced-motion preferences, and keep essential content and contact actions usable if WebGL is unavailable.

Make navigation, controls, and contact actions functional. Avoid dead buttons and forms that pretend to submit. Optimize rendering and assets so the experience stays responsive while scrolling and interacting.

Before calling it done, open the website in a real browser and inspect it at desktop and mobile sizes. Check all eight sections, the continuity of the 3D journey, typography, interactions, and loading behavior. Fix anything broken, generic, unreadable, or unfinished. Deliver the implemented website, not just a description of the concept.
